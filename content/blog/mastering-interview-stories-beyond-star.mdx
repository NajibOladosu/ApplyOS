STAR stands for Situation, Task, Action and Result. It gives an interview answer a clear order. The detail inside each part helps the interviewer understand how you work.

## Keep the situation short

Explain the problem and why it mattered. Avoid giving a full project history. The interviewer needs enough context to follow your decision.

## Name your responsibility

Say what you were responsible for and how it related to the team's work. Use “I” for your own actions and “we” for shared work.

## Explain a decision

Describe what you did and why. If you considered another option, explain the tradeoff. A debugging answer might cover how you reproduced the problem, which evidence changed your first theory and how you checked the fix.

## Describe the result you can support

Use measurements you actually have. If you didn't measure a percentage improvement, describe the observed outcome. You might have fixed a reproducible failure, documented a setup step or helped the team choose an approach.

Then add what you learned. Explain something you would repeat or change on the next project.

## A fictional example

> Our team kept getting different results when setting up a development environment. I took responsibility for investigating. I compared runtime versions and configuration, then reproduced the failure on a clean setup. I documented the required versions and added the missing setup step. A teammate followed the revised instructions successfully. Next time, I'd check the setup on a clean environment earlier.

Replace this example with your own experience and evidence before using it in an interview.
