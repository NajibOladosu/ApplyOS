Start with a few experiences you remember well. A difficult bug, a group project, a decision you changed or a piece of feedback you acted on can each become an interview example.

## Write down what actually happened

For each example, note the situation, your responsibility, the action you took and the result. Separate your contribution from the team's work. If you don't have a measured outcome, explain what you observed and what you learned.

Keep enough detail to answer follow-up questions. Why did you choose that approach? What other option did you consider? What would you change next time?

## Adapt the example to the question

The same project may contain examples of collaboration, problem-solving and learning. Choose the part that answers the question. Give enough context for someone who has never worked on your project, then spend most of the answer on your own decisions and actions.

If a question asks about experience you don't have, explain that honestly and discuss the closest relevant example.

Treat feedback as a prompt to revisit your answer. Check whether it addresses the question, makes your contribution clear and stays within facts you can explain. Keep your own judgement when a suggestion doesn't fit the experience.

## Practise a follow-up

After each answer, try another question about the same example. Explain an alternative you rejected, a tradeoff you made or something you learned later. This helps you understand the story well enough to discuss it naturally.
