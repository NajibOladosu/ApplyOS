A useful answer gives the interviewer a reason you chose this role and an example of what you could contribute. Start by reading the job description and the company's own website.

## Choose a specific reason

Pick a responsibility, product or problem that interests you. Explain why it connects to work you want to do. If you mention a company announcement, check the original source and its date.

## Connect that reason to your experience

Choose one relevant example from your work, studies or personal projects. Explain your part in it. Use numbers when you can support them with records. A clear description of what changed is useful when you don't have a measured result.

Here is a fictional structure to adapt using your own facts.

> I'm interested in this role because it involves building APIs that other teams rely on. In a recent project, I worked on request validation and made errors easier to understand. I'd like to bring that experience to your integration work and learn more about operating those services at scale.

## Make room for your own questions

Your preparation can also reveal things you need to ask about, such as the team's priorities, support for learning or expectations for the first few months. Keep those questions with the application so you can find them before the interview.
